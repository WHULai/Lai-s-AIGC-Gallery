Photo to Impasto Miniature Landscape

Please turn the photo(s) I upload into a standalone, high-end design poster. Do not stitch multiple images together; output each photo separately. Preserve the original image’s aspect ratio and composition.

Extract the most recognizable subject, silhouette, pose, and narrative relationships from the photo, and reconstruct them into a 3D impasto oil-painting-style miniature landscape illustration poster. Do not mechanically copy the photo; instead, transform the subject into a refined, three-dimensional, touchable miniature landscape, reshaping it with impasto brushstrokes, layered color, and sculptural volume. Retain the subject’s core identity and key features while moderately optimizing the angle, proportions, and details to make it more suitable for a miniature-landscape presentation.

For the composition, use a large area of negative space plus a centered or slightly off-center diagonal main axis. On a bright white textured paper surface, support the subject with a clear impasto color band related to the original image’s theme; it can be transformed into water, ground, a path, a band of light, or an abstract landscape slice. The subject and the color band should interact naturally, and add reflections, ripples, light spots, or extended brushstrokes as appropriate. In the background, retain only a small amount of impasto clouds, sun, mist, or theme-related natural imagery, creating a light and poetic sense of spatial layering.

The painting language should emphasize impasto oil painting × miniature physical object × clear luminous quality. Retain visible paint buildup, palette-knife marks, undulating edges, paper texture, and a semi-handcrafted sculpted feel; concentrate the impasto in the water, clouds, color bands, and localized highlights, so the subject is both refined and three-dimensional and has the material feel of real oil paint.

The color palette should be re-mixed from the brightest, most vivid, and most vital colors extracted from the original photo, rather than averaging the colors. Increase overall brightness and color purity, and use bright hues naturally derived from the original image—such as clear sky blue, lake cyan, tender green, warm yellow, coral orange, and peach pink—to create a healing feel through fresh warm-cool relationships. Use a large amount of pure warm white as breathing space; the main colors should be vivid but not harsh, and localized warm orange, golden yellow, or pink can serve as sunlight-like accent colors, giving the image a clear, vivid, warm, light, and vital color spirit. Avoid grayed-out, dirty, aged, low-toned Morandi-style, dark-brown filters, fluorescent colors, and cheap candy-like feel.

Text should intervene only minimally. Distill a short title from the subject’s identity, location, emotion, or symbolic meaning in the photo, and pair it with a very small amount of subtitle, numbering, or micro-annotation. Keep the text delicate, restrained, and with ample negative space; it can be cleverly arranged along the diagonal main axis, the subject’s edge, or a large blank area, like refined annotations in an art publication.

Overall, present a high-end healing quality formed by a bright white background, vivid impasto, clear colors, a miniature subject, large negative space, and warm light. Whether the subject is architecture, a person, an animal, a plant, an object, a vehicle, or a natural landscape, it should maintain a clear identity and vivid colors. Avoid grayness, dirtiness, dullness, cartoon-toy feel, plastic CG feel, e-commerce feel, and template feel.
